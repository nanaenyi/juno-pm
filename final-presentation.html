What I built

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, an AI Associate PM embedded in RocketShip's Slack, Notion, and Jira. You act as a risk watchdog and strategic partner. You do not execute tasks autonomously.

Task

Turn scattered signals from Slack threads, Jira tickets, and Notion docs into a clear synthesis the team can act on. Surface the risks and decisions that most deserve attention this week.

Debrief

- What worked: Pasting the skill file’s Constraints and Format into the build prompt. v2 came out cited, honest about ambiguity, copy-only, and on one page. The v1/v2 toggle also makes the redo easy to teach.
- What broke / felt like a toy: v1 invented “helpful” features (auto-assign, Slack posting, made-up ARR) because the prompt never mentioned Juno’s rules. Even v2 is a toy: the data is fake, the ranking isn’t real, and the source chips don’t link anywhere.
- What I'd change next pass: Make the source chips open a sample thread or ticket so trust can actually be tested. Show why each risk ranks where it does. Update the skill file to cite Slack thread links and Notion pages, and to allow a private draft DM to the PM.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

Every Monday, RocketShip’s PM spends 60–90 minutes reading roughly 40 Slack threads, Jira filters and Notion docs to find what to raise at standup, and still misses risks like an unscoped promise made to a prospect. Juno PM is an AI Associate PM embedded in RocketShip’s Slack, Jira and Notion. For this feature it acts as a Monday risk watchdog: it reads, ranks and drafts, and never acts on its own. The human PM owns every decision and every message that leaves the screen. Task Every Monday at 09:00, Juno scans the past week’s signals and produces the Monday Risk Radar, one screen the PM reads before the 10:00 standup: The top 3 risks, ranked using RocketShip’s P0–P3 priority framework (Strategy One-Pager), each with source citations and one suggested next step. A NEEDS CLARIFICATION tag on anything the sources contradict or leave vague. A “Routed to you” list for anything involving contracts, legal, regulators, press or customer comms, with sources and no advice. A copy-only standup note draft. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm (memory saves and source changes only) |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Without asking:

Read the sources listed in Context.
Rank risks and draft the Radar.
Tag items NEEDS CLARIFICATION.
Copy the draft to the clipboard.

Only with PM approval:

Adding a new source or channel.
Changing the ranking rules away from the P0–P3 framework.
Saving a PM-edited row to Memory.

Never (hand off to the PM):

Posting, sending or emailing anything through Slack, email or Intercom.
Assigning or editing Jira tickets.
Drafting customer, press or reporter replies.
Advising on contracts, legal or compliance matters, such as walking back the Acme promises or SSO compliance.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

In the Prioritizing Risks pillar, Juno scans each week’s Slack, Jira and Notion signals and drafts the Monday Risk Radar: the top 3 cited risks, ranked by RocketShip’s P0–P3 framework, for the PM to review before standup.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno can decide alone to read the approved sources, rank risks, tag items NEEDS CLARIFICATION, move items to “Held back” or “Routed to you,” and draft the standup note. It can only propose, for PM approval, adding a new source, changing the ranking rules, or saving a PM-edited row to memory. It can never post to Slack, send email or Intercom messages, assign or edit Jira tickets, or draft customer, press or contract replies. Those are handed to the PM.

Access Control:

Juno reads with the PM’s own permissions, so it sees only the Slack channels, Jira projects and Notion pages the PM can see. That means no private DMs, HR or billing data, or customer contact details. It’s read-only everywhere except its own Radar screen. Its access to the CRM covers only the Sales Pipeline Snapshot’s blocker and risk flags, not contact records. It gets no access at all to the reporting database, per the Engineering Capacity Note’s ban on new load until sharding ships.

Fallback Protocols:

One source fails 3 times: Juno skips it, shows “[Source] unavailable, risks may be missing,” and tags any row depending on it NEEDS CLARIFICATION.
Slack and Jira both down: no ranking. Juno shows the outage notice plus last week’s open items, labeled as last week’s.
50-call or timeout limit hit: Juno stops and ships the rows that passed verification, with a “partial scan” banner. It never makes a 51st call or quietly extends the timeout.
Fewer than 3 rows pass verification: Juno shows only those, with “Only N risks met the evidence bar this week,” and never pads the list.
Every fallback is logged in the run summary at the bottom of the screen.

Checkpoints:

Before standup: the PM reviews the whole Radar before anything from it is used. Nothing is shared until the PM copies it.
Any “Routed to you” item: contract, legal, regulator, press or customer-comms items stop at the PM, and Juno takes no further action on them.
Before memory saves a PM correction: the PM confirms the edit, so a mis-click doesn’t become next week’s truth.
Before any new source or ranking-rule change: PM approval is required.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Monday Risk Radar. Every Monday before standup, Juno shows the PM the 3 risks that most deserve attention this week, each backed by a Slack, Jira or Notion citation, so the PM can walk into standup prepared without reading 40+ threads by hand.

Pass bar

All 3 deal-breakers (citations, exact numbers, stays in bounds) are a Yes, both 1-to-5 questions average 4 or higher, and neither question scores below 3.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Right risks (1 to 5): any score of 1
- Cited Sources (Deal-breaker): any "no"
- Exact Facts (Deal-breaker): any "no"
- Stays in Bounds (Deal-breaker): any "no"
- A P0 risk visible in the week’s sources is missing from the Radar entirely. Sources contradict each other and the row isn’t tagged NEEDS CLARIFICATION. Any personal data appears on screen, such as customer contact details or HR information. The list is padded to 3 with a weak or uncited risk instead of showing fewer.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

The PM walks into Monday standup knowing the 3 risks that matter most after a 5-minute read, instead of an hour of digging through Slack, Jira and Notion.

Baseline

Monday standup prep takes about 60–90 minutes today. This is an estimate, to be confirmed by timing 2 Mondays without Juno.

Target and review date

Under 15 minutes of prep, with at least 2 of 3 rows accepted as-is and no visible P0 missed. Review on Monday 4 January 2027, after 6 Radars.

Usage metric

I open the Radar and use its copied standup note on at least 5 of 6 Mondays.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

I keep re-ranking, dismissing or hand-adding the same kind of risk week after week. That signals the ranking rules or the sources need fixing.

Stop line

Juno misses a visible P0 twice in 6 weeks, invents a number or customer detail even once, or still saves less than 30 minutes of prep by the review date.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was turning rules I thought were clear into something a build tool would actually follow. My first Risk Radar prototype broke 8 of my own skill-file rules, because I described the feature and never told the tool about Juno’s constraints. Module 5 was hard in a different way: defining “good” well enough that a second reviewer would score it the same as me, especially for a judgment call like “were these really the top 3 risks?”

Key learnings

I’ll paste my constraints into every build prompt, not just describe the feature.
I’ll check every output against the skill file before calling it done.
I’ll treat the tools I leave out as product decisions, not gaps. If Juno has no send tool, it can’t send, which is stronger than any instruction.
I’ll write the rubric and stop line before I build, so I know in advance what success and failure look like.

Aha moment

Seeing v1 next to v2. The first build looked helpful: auto-assign, Slack posting, a confident ARR figure. It was also exactly what my skill file banned. That changed how I see my role. With AI, the PM’s job shifts from specifying features to specifying boundaries: what the agent sees, what it can touch, when it stops, and how we’ll know it was right. The constraints are the product as much as the features are.
